SECTION 01

01Executive Summary

SpareParts today is feature-rich but navigation-heavy. The web app registers 74 screens in screen-registry.js, and the work of a single business moment — one client, one sale, one payment — is scattered across many of them. A counter sale lives in invoices-view.js, the client’s balance in contacts-view.js, their overdue buckets in customer-aging-view.js, their quotes in quotes-view.js, their reminders in two different screens, and payment receipt has no direct flow at all — the invoice list offers only View Profit / Send Invoice / Reminder actions, so a payment received after invoicing means a manual journal entry in Accounting.

Where time is lost today (from the code, not from opinion)

  • Fragmented client context. Contacts is a read-only two-column list capped at 100 rows with no search, no row click-through, and no create form. Answering “what does Elie owe and why?” takes three screens.
  • Dropdown-driven data entry. The POS customer picker is a plain <select> fed by /api/customers?page=1&pageSize=100; the part picker is a <select> over the entire catalog (api.list("/api/parts")). Every invoice line is a scroll-and-hunt exercise.
  • Dead-end search. The dashboard “Smart Search” input discards what you type — its Enter handler navigates to Inventory without passing the query — so every lookup is typed twice.
  • An action queue that can’t act. buildActionQueue() already computes 6 real business signals (negative P&L, unpaid receivables, red stock segments, currency-squeezed margins, failed messages, no-sales-yet), but caps at 4 items, links only to whole screens with no entity context, and forgets everything on refresh.
  • Blind messaging. Payment reminders fire with no preview; delivery failures surface only as a filtered list on the dashboard, disconnected from the client they belong to.

What the redesign changes

The Dashboard becomes a work tool. The Action Queue is promoted from a 4-item hint list to the organizing principle of the screen: every item is scored by severity × money at stake, has a lifecycle (appears → acted → resolved/dismissed), and deep-links into the exact action — not just a screen.

One screen per client. The Client Workspace merges Contacts + Customer Aging + per-screen invoice/quote/request lookups into a single customer-360 view where a payment can be received, an invoice raised, a quote drafted, and a WhatsApp reminder previewed and sent — without navigating away.

Keyboard-first speed. A global CtrlK palette with a terse grammar (inv eli → new invoice for Elie) plus barcode-wedge entry collapses the two highest-frequency tasks — counter sales and stock checks — to seconds.

Bottom line: across the 10 highest-frequency tasks, projected daily savings are ~47 min for counter staff, ~43 min for the owner, and ~8 min on quoting — roughly 1.6 hours per shop per day, or one full working month per year. Section 8 defines the time-on-task telemetry that will verify these numbers during the side-by-side rollout; no projection ships unmeasured.

SECTION 04

04The Action Queue as a Workflow Engine

What exists today — the honest baseline

buildActionQueue() in dashboard-view.js already detects six signals and renders them as cards. That is a genuinely good foundation — and exactly why the redesign keeps the concept and fixes the mechanics:

Today’s behavior (from code)ConsequenceIgnition change
Fixed insertion order; tasks.slice(0, 4) caps the listA $12 failed message can display while a $4,000 receivable is cut offScore-ranked list, no hard cap; overflow groups by category
Items carry only view: "accounting" etc.Deep link lands on a whole screen; user re-finds the entityDeep-link contract carries entity, tab, action, and a pre-drafted payload
Recomputed from scratch on every load; no stateNo memory of what was acted on or dismissed; same items nag dailyPersistent item identity: ruleKey + entityId; lifecycle survives refresh
Aggregate items only (“14 open transactions”)Not actionable without manual triagePer-entity items (“Garage Elie Motors · 3 invoices · $1,240”), worklist-able

Prioritization: severity × money at stake

Every item gets a score when signals are evaluated. Score determines order; nothing is silently dropped.

score = S × (1 + log₁₀(1 + money_at_stake_usd)) × A

S — severity class: S1 blocking/losing money now = 100 (negative daily P&L, day-close variance) · S2 money waiting = 60 (overdue receivables, quote-ready requests, currency-squeezed margins) · S3 hygiene = 30 (red stock segments, failed messages, expiring quotes) · S4 informational = 10 (no sales yet today).
money_at_stake — the receivable balance, quote value, or margin exposure attached to the entity; items with no monetary dimension use their category’s median so they are ordered, not zeroed.
A — age boost: +10% per day unresolved, capped at 2× — items that linger rise, they don’t fade.

Worked example: “Garage Elie Motors, 3 invoices 30+ days, $1,240” → 60 × (1 + log₁₀(1241)) × 1.0 ≈ 246. “1 failed WhatsApp message ($0 attached, category median $80)” → 30 × (1 + log₁₀(81)) ≈ 87. The receivable outranks the retry, in that order, every time — which matches how the owner actually triages.

SECTION 04 — CONTINUED

Item Lifecycle & Deep-Link Contract

FIG 5 · Action Queue item lifecycle. Persistence is keyed by ruleKey + entityId so acting or snoozing survives refresh and re-login.

The deep-link contract

Every queue item carries a structured target instead of today’s bare view string:

{ view: "client-workspace", entityId: 412, tab: "payments",
  action: "send-reminder", draft: { template: "AgingReminder", invoiceIds: [8811, 8873, 8901] } }

  • view + entityId — lands inside the entity, not on a list screen. “3 invoices overdue for Garage Elie Motors” opens that client’s workspace, payments tab — never the generic aging table.
  • action + draft — the target screen opens with the action sheet already populated: reminder text drafted with the exact invoice numbers, payment sheet pre-filled with the balance, reorder list pre-checked.
  • Round trip — completing the action emits an event (queue_item_acted); the next signal evaluation flips the item to RESOLVED if the underlying condition cleared. The queue is therefore a closed loop: it can tell the owner not just what needs doing, but what got done today — which becomes the day-close summary.

SECTION 05

05Client Workspace — Operational Flows

The Client Workspace replaces three partial views — Contacts (read-only list, no search, no detail), Customer Aging (buckets without invoices), and per-screen lookups in POS/Sales, Quotes and Part Requests — with one screen per client: identity header (name, phone, WhatsApp status, balance, aging pill), an action bar (New Invoice · Receive Payment · New Quote · Send Reminder), and tabs (Overview · Invoices · Payments · Quotes · Requests · Messages · Activity). Four flows carry the daily load:

1 · Receive payment without leaving the screen

Action bar → payment sheet slides over the tab area. Amount defaults to full balance; typing a partial amount shows oldest-first allocation across open invoices, editable per line. Confirm posts the journal entry behind the scenes (same double-entry the manual journal produces today — cash debit, A/R credit), prints a receipt, and the header balance + aging pill update in place. 4 steps, ~30 s, versus a 12-step manual journal today.

2 · Invoice from client context

“New Invoice” opens the same keyboard-first POS draft as Ctrl+K inv, with the client pre-filled — no customer dropdown at all (today’s POS makes the operator scroll a <select> capped at 100 rows even when they arrived from a client’s record). On post, the invoice appears at the top of the Invoices tab and the balance updates without a reload.

3 · Aging-driven collection worklist

The Payments tab shows the client’s open invoices aged inline (the join the owner does by hand today between customer-aging-view and invoices-view). In worklist mode — entered from the queue item or the aging screen’s “Start collections” — the workspace advances client-to-client sorted by bucket × amount, logging an outcome per client: reminder sent · promise-to-pay (date) · dispute · unreachable. Outcomes feed the next day’s queue: a broken promise re-queues at higher score.

4 · Communication follow-ups with templates

The Messages tab is the client’s whole thread: invoices sent, reminders, quote follow-ups — with delivery status per message, replacing today’s blind fire-and-forget (aging’s “Send Reminder” posts accountBalanceReminder with no preview; failures surface only on the dashboard). Templates — AgingReminder, InvoiceCopy, QuoteFollowUp, RequestQuote — render with live data for preview and edit before send. A failed message shows inline with one-click retry, and raises an S3 queue item pointing back to this exact thread.

06Speed Mechanics

Global CtrlK command palette — the grammar

One input, available on every screen, replacing the dashboard’s query-discarding search box. Verbs are 1–4 characters; arguments are fuzzy-matched with type-ahead. Enter always executes the top match; results render inline where the answer is informational (stock/price) instead of navigating.

Keyboard-first counter sale

The POS draft is an input loop, not a form: scan field → qty → scan field. Tab/Enter only; the mouse is never required. Price edits are F4 on the focused line; tender defaults to exact total and cash (today’s free-text “payment method” input becomes a 1-key choice: Cash / Transfer / on Account). Target: 2-line sale in ~30 s (Fig 1).

Barcode flow tie-in

Scanners act as keyboard wedges: a scan anywhere in the palette or the POS scan field resolves by barcode → internal code → OEM code (the same identifiers the dashboard already lists as search tabs). A scan on the dashboard opens the part card; a scan inside a draft adds a line. The existing Barcode Mode screen (barcode-mode-view.js) remains for camera-based scanning on mobile.

Recents & favorites

The palette’s empty state shows the last 5 clients, last 5 parts, and pinned actions — because counter traffic is heavily repeat business, most lookups become two keystrokes (Ctrl+K, ↵). Recents are per-user and local-first, so they cost no API round-trip.

Latency budget

Palette matching runs against a cached client + part index (the app already ships the full parts list to the browser via api.list("/api/parts") on dashboard and POS load — Ignition reuses that as a versioned cache instead of a per-screen refetch). Budget: <50 ms keystroke-to-result on cached data; stock quantities revalidate async and patch the card.

Why keyboard-first pays for itself: T2 + T6 run ~55 times a day. Every second shaved off their loop saves nearly a minute a day; the palette shaves ~65 s and ~12 s respectively. No other single mechanism in this package moves the daily total more.

SECTION 07

07Cross-Device Workflow

What the owner checks from a phone

The owner’s phone sessions are short, frequent, and read-mostly-plus-approve. The mobile web layout keeps exactly two blocks above the fold:

  • KPI band — Sales today, Profit today, Cash, Overdue receivables. Four numbers, full-width, tap-to-drill.
  • Action Queue — the same scored list as desktop. Phone-appropriate actions per item: approve a pre-drafted reminder (one tap — the draft was composed by the deep-link contract, not on the phone), snooze, or “flag for desk” which pins the item to the top of the desktop queue.

Also phone-friendly: receiving a payment (the 4-step sheet works one-handed), checking a part’s stock/price via the palette in search-first mode, and the day-close summary card in the evening.

Offline & poor connectivity at the shop counter

Reality: counter connectivity drops in exactly the moments that matter (a queue of customers). The failure mode to design out is “the sale stops because the spinner spins.”

  • Read path is cache-first. The parts index (already fully client-side today) and the client directory are versioned local caches. Stock lookups and price checks answer instantly from cache with a “as of 10:42” badge; a background revalidation patches quantities when the network returns.
  • Write path is queue-and-sync. Invoice posts, payment receipts and reminder sends enqueue locally with an optimistic receipt (marked “pending sync”). On reconnect, the queue drains in order; the server remains authoritative — a stock rejection (the same validation /api/sales performs today) surfaces as a red queue item on the dashboard rather than a silent loss.
  • Barcode keeps working. Scans resolve against the local index, so the input loop never blocks on network.
  • Visible state, no mystery. A connectivity chip in the command bar shows pending-sync count; tapping it lists queued writes. Nothing auto-retries invisibly past 3 attempts without surfacing.
  • Out of scope for offline: journal entries, conversions, and anything multi-user-sensitive — these fail fast with a clear “needs connection” message instead of queuing.

Cross-device rule of thumb: the phone decides and approves; the desktop composes and executes. Both share one Action Queue state, so an item acted on from the phone is resolved on the desktop and vice versa — one to-do list for the business, not one per device.

08Adoption & Rollout

Side-by-side period (weeks 1–4)

The new Dashboard and Client Workspace register as additional screens alongside the existing ones — the screen-registry pattern makes this a two-line addition, and legacy screens stay reachable via Ctrl+K > screen-name throughout. Week 1–2: new screens are opt-in (a “Try the new dashboard” banner). Week 3–4: new screens become the default; legacy screens remain one command away. Removal of the legacy Contacts/Aging screens happens only after the success gate below is met — not on a calendar date.

Closing

The redesign wins not by adding capability — nearly every flow in this report re-uses an API the app already has — but by collapsing distance: between a signal and its action (Action Queue deep links), between a client and everything about them (Client Workspace), and between an intent and its execution (Ctrl+K). The projections total ~98 minutes a day; the telemetry in Section 8 exists so that six weeks after rollout, that number is no longer a projection.
