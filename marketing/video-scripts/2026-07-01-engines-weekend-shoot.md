## Before you film — read this once

- **These are NOT alternators.** Full engine assemblies sitting on an engine stand or pallet. Do not pick one up, do not tilt the stand for a shot, do not ask anyone else to lift or carry it for camera. All "handling" in these scripts is Ralph's hand pointing at or resting on a component — never lifting the engine itself.
- **Orientation:** Vertical (9:16), phone held upright. Lock focus/exposure by tapping the engine on screen before you start talking.
- **Lighting:** Film near a shop bay door or window in daylight if possible — engines are bigger and darker than an alternator, so they need more light to read clearly on camera. If shooting indoors, turn on every shop light you have and shoot with the engine facing the light source, not backlit. A shadow-heavy engine reads as "hidden condition" to a buyer — avoid that.
- **Steady:** These shots are mostly walk-around, so handheld is fine — but slow down. Walk at half the speed that feels natural; fast walk-arounds look shaky and cheap on a big-ticket item. Brace elbows against your body for close-ups on components.
- **Space:** Clear a walking path around the stand before you hit record — nobody wants to see Ralph step over a bucket mid-shot. Move loose clutter out of frame, but keep the real shop background (racks, stands, other cores) — that's on-brand, not something to hide.
- **Background:** Real shop is fine and expected. Just make sure nothing is leaning against the engine itself or blocking the components you're pointing at.
- **Audio:** Talk close to the phone mic (arm's length or less). Shop noise carries more around a big open bay — if it's loud, do text-overlay + voiceover recorded in a quieter corner instead of live sync sound.
- **Do NOT say "runs," "tested," "compression-tested," or "bench-tested."** There is no confirmed test data for any of these five. Do not imply the engine has been started or verified to run.
- **Safe language to use instead:** "pulled from a running vehicle" (only if that's a reasonable inference from the salvage source — treat all five as inferred-running pulls, not confirmed), "complete assembly," "clean visual condition," "low visible wear," "all major components present." Stick to what the camera can actually show — visual completeness and condition claims only, never functional guarantees.
- **Do NOT state a price on camera or in on-screen text.** No dollar figures anywhere in these scripts — Ralph hasn't set pricing yet. Every script ends on "message for price" or equivalent.
- **One-off urgency is real and matters more here.** Each of these is a single salvage engine — once it's sold, there's no second unit and likely no direct replacement for months. Say so plainly. Don't oversell it with hype language — the size of the investment sells the urgency on its own.
- **Tone shift from the alternators:** This is a serious buy for someone planning an engine swap, not an impulse "add to cart." Hook and voiceover should sound confident and matter-of-fact — informative, not hyped. No fast cuts, no "OMG" energy. Let the size and completeness of the engine do the selling.
- **Scale and proof shots matter more here.** Because a buyer can't hold this part in their hands before buying, the shot list leans on: full walk-around for scale, hand pointing at intake manifold / injectors / wiring harness / turbo (if visible) to show completeness, a shot with a tape measure or a person standing near it for scale reference, and a close-up on any visible casting number or VIN/build tag proving fitment to the stated vehicle.

Each script below is self-contained: film it, no editing skill needed beyond trimming clips together in order and adding the text overlays listed.

## Filming order suggestion (for one weekend session)

These take longer per unit than the alternators — budget more time per engine, and don't try to rush the walk-arounds.

1. Scout and confirm a clear walking path around each engine stand before filming starts — move clutter, not the engine.
2. Set up the phone rig once per engine location (propped for the talking-to-camera shot); walk-around and pointing shots are handheld — brace and slow down.
3. Film all 5 wide establishing + walk-around shots first, moving stand to stand.
4. Film all 5 "hand points to components" shots next (intake/injectors/harness) — keep the pointing order consistent across all 5 for a clean edit later.
5. Film all 5 casting-number/tag close-ups.
6. Film all 5 talking-to-camera segments back to back to stay in the same calm, confident tone.
7. Film all 5 scale/"1 in stock" close-outs.
8. Sort footage into 5 folders by vehicle before handing off for editing — label folders clearly since engine footage looks more similar across units than the alternators did.

## Safety note — filming around a heavy engine on a stand

- **Never lift or tilt the engine for a shot.** All camera moves are the phone/Ralph moving around the engine, not the engine moving. If a stand has a tilt function built in and Ralph already uses it safely for shop work, a slight tilt for one static shot is fine — but that's a normal shop operation Ralph would do anyway, not something staged for camera. Don't add tilting or repositioning specifically to get a better angle.
- **Don't ask anyone else to help lift, push, or reposition the stand for a shot.** If a different angle is needed, walk the phone around the engine instead of moving the engine.
- **Keep the walking path clear** before recording so nobody is stepping backward near the stand while filming — falls near heavy equipment are the real risk here, not the engine itself.
- **Don't stage a person under, over, or leaning on the engine** for a scale shot — standing beside it at a safe distance is enough to convey size.
- **If a stand looks unstable, unbalanced, or is on an incline, don't film around it until it's secured.** Not worth a shot.
